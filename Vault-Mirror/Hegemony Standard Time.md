## Why Divide the Hour by Ten

The 60-minute hour is a relic of Babylonian mathematics. HST replaces it with **10 Moments per hour (1 Moment = 6 minutes)**.

- **Decimal Efficiency** — mirrors metric and financial systems; easier mental math, cleaner ledgers.
- **Ledger Resolution** — six minutes is fine enough to audit any mode of attention without forcing false interruption on deep or monitoring work. The Moment is the *ledger grain*, not the attention cycle. Thresholds are **expressed in Moments, not equal to one**: flow entry is 3–4 Moments (18–24 min), a Pomodoro cycle is 5 Moments, a Deep Flow block is ≈25 Moments.
- **Command Synchronization** — ten ticks per hour simplifies operational tempo.
- **Doctrinal Cohesion** — the Tenfold Order.
- **Economic Logic** — Time = Money = Attention = Resource.

## The Standard Moment

**1 Moment = (6 minutes + Focused Decision) ÷ Resistance**

The smallest measurable unit of attention expenditure. Each logged Moment records when you acted, what it was aimed at, and how much Resistance was overcome. This is temporal bookkeeping, not time tracking.

### The Crossing Decomposes

The Moment measures the crossing. The crossing itself has named parts. *(See [[Time and Fire]].)*

| Object | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Run** | The engagement, whole — from sighting the Target to termination. One pass of PTRSGA at task scale. The thing that gets logged. |
| **Delay** | Ready + Set. Staging, crossing, gathering, opening, closing out. Produces nothing. |
| **Work** | Go. Hands on it. The productive span. |
| **Leg** | Delay when the crossing is spatial. A Delay with a map reference. |

**Run = Delay + Work.**

Delay is the actionable component. Most overrun is Delay that was never counted, not Work that took longer than forecast.

**Two diagnostic ratios:**

| Ratio | Reads |
|---|---|
| **Estimated Duration ÷ Duration** | Forecast error. Corrects the library. |
| **Delay ÷ Work** | Composition. High ratio means the problem is staging, not effort. |

The first says *whether* the forecast was wrong. The second says *where*.

**Resistance is not currently logged.** The Watch Log records actions; it records neither Duration nor Resistance. A Moment has two values — **face value** (6 minutes, fixed) and **purchasing power** (what the crossing accomplishes, variable and inverse to Resistance). Logging Duration alone builds a *duration* library. Logging Duration **and** Resistance builds a **purchasing-power** library, which is what forecasting actually needs. *(See [[Time and Fire]], Open Item 1 — the highest-value unbuilt item in the system.)*

## Where your terms land

**Timeline** — the whole span of the operation. Ordered, with Phases as its divisions.

**Phase** — a bounded division of the Timeline, with conditions that open and close it. A Block at operational scale. Same object, bigger altitude.

**Block** — a Phase at task scale. Claimed Interval, two declared edges. *(Formerly "Window" — retired 120260813.)*

**Docket** — Tasks owed but not yet phased. The unassigned backlog. Assignment happens twice: item → Phase, then item → Block inside the Phase.

**Deadline** — a Decisive Point, or the closing condition on a Phase. Not an Interval. A Position.

**Order** — the vehicle. WARNORD/OPORD/FRAGO. You already run this.

## The Day-Split System — Choosing the Watch

*Ratified 120260803. Supersedes the 10-hour Watch.*

**A Watch is not a fixed length. A Watch is one division of the Day.** The Day is fixed at 240 Moments; how it is cut is a command decision set by the coverage the assignment requires — not by preference.

| Split | Watches/Day | Length        | Moments     | Name & Use                                                                                 |
| ----- | ----------- | ------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| ÷2    | 2           | 12 hr         | 120         | **Two-Section.** Max coverage, min crew, highest burn. Emergency and wartime only.         |
| ÷3    | 3           | 8 hr          | 80          | **Standard.** The three-shift day. **Praetor default.**                                    |
| ÷4    | 4           | 6 hr          | 60          | **Four-Section.** Sustainable rotation; requires depth of crew.                            |
| ÷6    | 6           | 4 hr          | 40          | **Six-Section.** Short Watches for high-vigilance duty — the decrement counter.            |
| ÷7    | 7           | 5×4hr + 2×2hr | 5×40 + 2×20 | **Rotating Split.** Deliberately unequal. The odd count walks the roster forward each Day. |
| ÷12   | 12          | 2 hr          | 20          | **Twelve-Section.** Ceremonial or extreme-vigilance. Rarely warranted.                     |

### The selection rule is attentional, not preferential

Watch length is set by the **attention mode the duty demands** ([[The Attention Codex]] Part I):

- **Vigilance duty** — monitoring, security, driving → **÷6 or ÷4.** The decrement is time-on-task driven and cannot be broken by rest *inside* the task, only by ending the Watch. **Short Watches are the countermeasure.**
- **Pomodoro duty** — routine execution → **÷3 or ÷4.**
- **Deep Flow duty** — doctrine, design, high-skill build → **÷3 or ÷2.** The 3–4 Moment entry ramp must be amortised over a long block to be worth paying.
- **Fairness constraint dominant** → **÷7 Rotating Split.**

> **The Rotating Split Principle.** When a duty has a worst Hour, divide the Day into an *odd* number of Watches so the worst Hour moves. Equal division is fair to the clock. Unequal division is fair to the Crew.

### Coverage math — why three crews is not enough

A Day needs 3 Watches covered. A Week needs **21**.

- 3 crews → 7 Watches each → **56 hr/week.** Covers the clock, burns the crew.
- Sustainable coverage at a 5-Watch (40 hr) Cycle requires **21 ÷ 5 = 4.2 crews.**

Real 24/7 operations run four or five crews, never three. **Three crews cover the clock; they do not cover it sustainably.** Budget the fourth crew or budget the burnout.

### The Utilization Ceiling

The purse is 160 Moments. **The bookable fraction is not 160.**

Queue wait time does not rise linearly with load. It scales as ρ/(1−ρ) — Kingman's formula. Arithmetic, not psychology.

| Booked | Wait, as multiple of service time |
|---|---|
| 50% | ~1× |
| 80% | ~4× |
| 90% | ~9× |
| 95% | ~19× |

**The operator is a single server.** Every Task, RV, and Obligation passes through the same operator, serially. The Day is not a container to be filled — it is a machine with one spindle.

**Ceiling: ~80% of bookable Moments**, computed against the Watch being booked — never against the 240-Moment Day, which counts the Sleep Watch as capacity.

| Frame | Available | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| Work Watch | 80 | **64 Moments** |
| Waking Day | 160 | **128 Moments** |

Anything above is not ambition. It is queue collapse — one slip cascades because there is nowhere for it to go. Unbooked capacity is not slack to be eliminated; it is structurally required by any queue with variable service times.

### Amendment — 120260803: Drift moved from Target to Perceive; Entropy takes its slot

The old focus-of-effort triad read *Adversary / Friction / Drift* — which listed one enemy, one instrument, and one symptom as though they were three targets of the same kind.

- **Drift is what Perceive finds.** It is the symptom. You detect it; you do not attack it.
- **Target names the cause and picks the fight.** Three sources of loss at one logical level:

| Focus of effort | What it is | Signature | Attack by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adversary** | Loss caused by an agent with intent | Something is being done *to* you, on a schedule not yours | Raise Friction on his approach; counter-salience; deny the Seat |
| **Friction** | Loss caused by the cost of motion being set wrong | Everything takes longer than it should; you avoid tasks by overhead, not difficulty | Lower it where it works against you — route, batch, pre-stage, automate |
| **Entropy** | Loss with no agent — decay by default | Nothing attacked you; it simply degraded. Stale logs, unmaintained Stores, lapsed rhythm | Maintenance, audit, re-issue. The Watch Log exists for this |

**Why Entropy takes the third slot:** it was already the denominator of the Doctrinal Equation and was the one source of loss the triad never named. The Adversary acts with intent. Friction is a dial set wrong. Entropy needs neither an enemy nor an error; it only needs time. **Most Hours are lost to the third and blamed on the first.**

### Behavioral Rationale

PERCEIVE **detects** Drift — anchors the loop in reality, not assumption; the field includes the operator. TARGET **classifies its cause** and converts attention into intent. READY transforms Resistance into a rehearsed script. SET locks the go/no-go threshold. GO channels focus into measurable production. AAR locks the rhythm.

**1 Watch = 8 Hours = 8 PTRSG Loops.** Each Watch produces one high-value deliverable (T3).
